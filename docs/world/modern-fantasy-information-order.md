# Modern fantasy and unequal public knowledge

**Status:** Accepted setting direction, 2026-11-04. Detailed institutions and political arrangements remain emergent.

The five starting regions represent cultural centers, **not** exclusive races, character classes, technological ages or fixed ability restrictions. Modern human society can include ordinary people, cultivators, mages, superpowered people, scientists and hybrids; all are subject to the same objective world laws.

## Ordinary life
Modern settlements may feature communications networks, universities, hospitals, commerce, transport, governments, research institutions, social media and journalism. Public knowledge can include well-documented unusual abilities while the deeper cosmology, rare techniques and clandestine actors remain uncertain or inaccessible.

## Information layers
1. World truth: authoritative events, abilities and material consequences.
2. Direct observations: witness-specific, potentially fallible.
3. Evidence: measurements, recordings, artifacts and independently verifiable records.
4. Public information: journalism, education, rumors, propaganda and widely available records.
5. Restricted knowledge: classified archives, secret sect teachings, private research, personal memories and inaccessible evidence.
6. Beliefs: individual interpretations and confidence, which may contradict reality.

Access is determined by actual channels, institutions, trust, literacy, resources, technologies, legal powers and personal relationships—not omniscience or region membership.

## Public-order organizations
A modern institution may investigate anomalies, protect civilians, manage crises, coordinate with gifted individuals, withhold sensitive findings or conceal events. It has budgets, personnel, incentives, oversight, internal disagreements and failure modes. Its actions can be ethical, abusive, protective, corrupt or contradictory. No organization has guaranteed secrecy, total control or privileged access to the engine's truth.

## Emergent tensions
Accidental exposures, conflicting recordings, public skepticism, scientific replication, leaked archives, political pressure, whistleblowers, misinformation and inter-cultural migration can change what society knows. A public incident may be contained, misunderstood, openly accepted or trigger broad cultural transformation.

## Modeling constraints
- Preserve objective causality: suppression of information does not erase an event.
- Record information transmission as events with sources, recipients, permissions and timestamps.
- Represent secrecy as a contingent property of access and evidence, not a magical global visibility switch.
- Do not make modern technology exclusive to the science region or cultivation exclusive to the cultivation region.
- Let institutions evolve rather than predetermining that the modern world remains ignorant forever.
