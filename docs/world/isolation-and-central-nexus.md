# Cultural isolation, contested borders and the central nexus

**Status:** Accepted design direction (2026-11-04); all illustrative motivations remain emergent rather than fixed canon.

## Principle
The five starting civilizations may be separated by geography, information control, laws, economics, ecological hazards, resource scarcity, personal ambition, historical trauma, or sincere protective beliefs. No single omnipotent conspiracy or global barrier is assumed. Every region has internal diversity, factions, competing beliefs and unequal access to knowledge. Cultural origin is never a fixed ability class.

## Possible pressures
- Modern society: secrecy institutions, public skepticism, scientific uncertainty, legal restrictions.
- Cultivation society: sect monopolies, control of techniques, spiritual-resource dependence, inherited taboos.
- Magical society: warded borders, guild regulation, feared magical contamination, rival schools.
- Primal society: ecological stewardship, ancestral obligations, terrain, intergroup politics.
- Superpowered society: registration, elite power monopolies, risk management, rights movements.

These are optional seeded factions and conditions, not universal properties of their populations.

## Crossing and central nexus
The central region is accessible in principle but reaching it may be difficult. Individual travelers can be exiles, defectors, explorers, merchants, refugees, researchers, diplomats, or ordinary migrants. Success depends on actual travel routes, resources, information, abilities, laws, weather and opposition.

The nexus can accumulate mixed institutions, trade, inventions, conflicts and alliances. It is **not** automatically an arena or a predetermined tournament. Competition arises from incentives and encounters, while peaceful cooperation is equally possible.

## Simulation requirements
- Borders have variable permeability, enforcement budgets, routes, hazards and corruption.
- Institutions have conflicting goals, limited personnel, budgets and fallible intelligence.
- Agent beliefs about outsiders can differ from objective world state.
- Discovery, migration, smuggling, diplomacy, disclosure and conflict are causal events.
- Isolation can strengthen, collapse, re-form or evolve as a result of events.
- Agents can learn any tradition if conditions permit; mixed-culture families and institutions are possible.

## MVP test scenarios
1. A cultivator learns of a hidden route and crosses a border despite sect restrictions.
2. A mage receives a false rumor about outsiders and changes travel plans.
3. A public-order organization fails to prevent a leak because witnesses retain independent evidence.
4. A refugee reaches the nexus and shares a technique; subsequent knowledge propagation is source-tracked.
5. Two seeded worlds with different institutional incentives develop different migration patterns.
